**Operator and provider slot mathematics research**

Research date: 2026-10-22. This document distinguishes published requirements and platform behavior from recommendations for Squid Surf Slot. It does not establish certification, platform approval, or access to another provider's proprietary mathematics.

**Who determines a result**

Stake hosts games from external studios as well as Stake Originals and Engine publishers. Its current explanation expressly distinguishes external providers' own technology and RNG systems from Stake's verification system. Listing a slot on Stake does not automatically make it provably fair. [Stake explanation](https://stake.com/blog/provably-fair-explanation)

Rainbet says its Originals support individual bet verification, while third-party slots and live games are handled by their providers. Another help article says provider systems operate independently and are outside Rainbet's control. These are operator statements, not independent audits of every listed game; the article's broad description of provider systems as provably fair should not be treated as proof that every supplier exposes cryptographic verification. [Rainbet product distinction](https://help.rainbet.com/en/articles/15253332-why-players-trust-rainbet), [Rainbet verification explanation](https://help.rainbet.com/en/articles/15267252-provably-fair)

A concrete architecture is available in Engine's wallet documentation: the remote gaming server communicates with the operator's wallet, authenticates sessions, initiates rounds with a debit, and completes rounds with settlement. Its operator integration examples cover authenticated wallet requests, transaction replay, reversals, and transaction ordering. This separates game-result generation from account balances without removing the need for correct integration. [Wallet API](https://stake-engine.com/docs/rgs/wallet), [Official operator integration examples](https://github.com/engineio/integration)

**Two valid mathematical architectures**

Engine's mathematics documentation contrasts conventional real-time RGS logic with its own finite outcome approach. Conventional logic can select reel stops with a cryptographically secure RNG, construct the board, evaluate wins, and execute bonus rules. Engine instead publishes recorded rounds and a weighted lookup table. The RGS selects a weighted outcome and returns its event sequence for presentation. [Engine mathematics](https://stake-engine.com/docs/math)

These architectures must not be confused. A finite published model can be an authored game distribution in its own right; it does not have to reproduce the natural frequencies of an earlier simulation. Engine explicitly describes changing weights during development to obtain the intended distribution. Its quick-start documentation also describes deliberately producing more rare outcomes before optimization. That is offline model construction. It is different from modifying odds during a player's session to recover previous payouts. [Distribution optimization paper](https://stake-engine.com/docs-content/distribution_optimization.pdf), [Math quick start](https://stake-engine.com/docs/math/quick-start)

Recommendation: retain a clear, versioned definition of which architecture Squid Surf Slot implements. A traditional model needs explicit reel strips or probability tables and deterministic evaluation. An Engine export needs complete, coherent event books whose payouts match their lookup rows. Neither model should use player balance, deposits, recent wins, or measured session RTP to alter the next draw.

**Provably fair does not mean equal payout probabilities**

Stake's current Engine fairness page says compatible stateless games publish every possible result and its selection probability. A cryptographic calculation using client and server seeds selects a published result; revealing the server seed permits retrospective verification. This means the old claim that only Stake Originals have provably fair verification is now incomplete. Coverage should still be checked for the particular product and version. [Engine fairness](https://stake-engine.com/fair)

Stake's Originals implementation documents server seed, client seed, nonce, and cursor inputs. Verification establishes that an outcome follows the committed process. It does not imply a zero house edge or equal probabilities for every displayed symbol or prize. [Originals implementation](https://stake.com/provably-fair/implementation)

For this project, keep the uniform random source separate from the mapping into deliberately weighted game outcomes. Seeded reproducibility is useful for development, but a reproducible generator alone does not establish secure production randomness or a complete commitment-and-verification protocol.

**Fairness standards and their limits**

UKGC RTS 7A requires acceptable randomness, unpredictable generation, and outcomes distributed according to theoretical probabilities. It prohibits compensated behavior. RTS 7B requires the implementation to follow disclosed rules and paytables, including correct mapping of random inputs. RTS 7C rejects substituted near-miss displays. RTS 7D restricts changes to rules, payouts, and probabilities while a game is available, while allowing properly described bonus-state rules. Therefore independence should not be misread as forbidding persistent wilds or changing bonus multipliers: those can be explicit state transitions rather than hidden compensation. [UKGC RTS 7](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/standards/remote-gambling-and-software-technical-standards/rts-7-generation-of-random-outcomes)

GLI-19 v3.0 is the directly relevant interactive-gaming reference. Sections 3.2 and 4.5 cover RNG review, scaling, independence, available outcomes, and outcome selection without adaptive discarding. Sections 4.4.1 and 4.4.3 require clear award, multiplier, and payline rules. Section 1.5.2 recognizes operator-selected configurations and testing against the intended production configuration. GLI-11 v3.0 covers gaming devices; section 3.2.5 similarly requires available outcomes with appropriate likelihood independent of prior draws, except where game design specifies otherwise. These are technical references, not evidence that this project or any named operator holds certification under them. [GLI-19](https://gaminglabs.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/GLI-19-Interactive-Gaming-Systems-v3.0.pdf), [GLI-11](https://gaminglabs.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/GLI-11-Gaming-Devices-V3-0.pdf)

**Settlement, units, and mode costs**

Recommended settlement model: freeze the accepted base stake, mode, cost multiplier, and math version at round creation. Let base stake be B, mode cost C, and final payout multiplier P measured against B. The debit is B × C, the gross award is B × P, and net change is B × (P − C). A 100× bonus purchase returning 80× base stake is an 80% return on purchase cost. It is not an 80× profit. Include all retriggers and free spins belonging to that purchase in its completed-round award.

Engine's published file format declares a cost multiplier per mode and requires matching payouts between books and lookup tables. Its replay documentation separately exposes payout and cost multipliers and asks the client to show real bet cost. Do not assume identically named fields have identical units across publication files, runtime responses, and replay APIs. [Official math file specification](https://github.com/engineio/docs/blob/main/src/routes/faq/math/what-files-are-required/%2Bpage.svx), [Replay requirements](https://stake-engine.com/docs/approval-guidelines/game-replay-requirements)

Engine monetary API values use integers with six decimal places: 1,000,000 represents one currency unit. Currency formatting is a presentation concern. Recommendation: use explicitly named integer amounts and payout scales, check safe numeric ranges, define rounding once, and reconcile one authoritative settlement per round. Preserve an unfinished round for resume instead of generating another result after reconnect. [Official currency specification](https://github.com/engineio/docs/blob/main/src/routes/docs/reference/currencies/%2Bpage.svx)

**Exact RTP, simulation, and validation**

For a complete fixed distribution with weights w and base-stake payout multipliers P, expected payout is sum(w × P) / sum(w). For a constant mode cost C, RTP is that expectation divided by C. This is an exact property of the published table, subject to its actual numeric representation. It does not prove that the event presentation or wallet integration is correct. Engine's optimization paper explicitly derives the weighted expectation. [Distribution mathematics](https://stake-engine.com/docs-content/distribution_optimization.pdf)

For real-time mathematics, exhaustive enumeration or analytical state calculations can establish exact components; Monte Carlo estimates the remainder. Do not label a simulated total exact because the base-game component was enumerated. Recommended reporting includes complete paid-round sample count, seed, actual turnover, gross return, standard deviation, uncertainty interval, feature frequencies, cap frequency, and contribution by feature and mode.

For independent equal-cost complete rounds, an approximate 95% interval for mean return is the sample mean plus or minus 1.96 × sample standard deviation / square root of N. Rare large awards can make short samples misleading, and dependent free spins should not be counted as separate independent paid rounds. UKGC's monitoring guidance ties tolerance to volatility and sample size, gives 95% examples, and recommends considering stake-level and channel differences. Its monitoring tolerances diagnose performance; they are not a license to adjust outcomes to force convergence. [UKGC RTP monitoring](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/licensees-and-businesses/guide/live-return-to-player-performance-monitoring-of-games-of-chance)

UKGC testing distinguishes RNG review from game mathematics, implementation, mapping, simulation, emulation, and manual checking. BMM asks for reel strips, weights, paytables, caps, per-state prize probabilities, simulation statistics, PAR sheets, and production configuration. eCOGRA likewise separates RNG, payout mathematics, bonus behavior, simulations, and integration checks. These support validating more than an average RTP number. [UKGC testing procedure](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/strategy/testing-strategy-for-compliance-with-remote-gambling-and-software-technical/3-procedure-for-testing), [BMM mathematics requirements](https://bmm.com/bmm-mathematics/), [eCOGRA engine testing](https://ecogra.org/services/casino-testing-services-game-engine-certification/)

**What cannot be inferred**

Public rules, RTP, maximum win, and volatility descriptions do not uniquely identify proprietary reel strips, feature weights, or multiplier frequencies. As a simple mathematical example, a 48% chance of 2× and a 9.6% chance of 10× both produce 96% RTP with otherwise zero payouts, but very different experiences. No consulted source provides the exact private mathematics for every Pragmatic Play, Hacksaw, Nolimit City, or Rainbet provider title. Engine's published compatible-game distributions are a specific exception, not a basis for claiming all providers use the same implementation.

The supported goal is an original, internally consistent model with declared rules and measurable behavior. Reproducing an unspecified commercial provider's exact mathematics would require its actual authorized model data.
